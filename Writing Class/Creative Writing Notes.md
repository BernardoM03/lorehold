Violence is the easy answer because it makes the scene's meaning obvious. A man dies, so we know it mattered.
### How to Write Deeper
**1. What does the character want in the next minute, and what is stopping them?** Answer in a short sentence. If your answer is abstract, you'll stall again. "Respect" produces nothing. "To get out of the room without being asked a second time" produces a door, a distance, a man between him and it.

**2. What is the character doing with their hands?** This is the cheapest reliable source of detail in prose and it's underused. A man in a bar wanting to leave does something with a cup, a strap, a coin, the edge of a table.

**3. What does the character look at that he shouldn't, and what do they avoid looking at?** Attention is characterized by what it refuses as much as what it lands on. 

**4. What would they notice that nobody else in the room would?** This is the one that produces character rather than scenery. Take for instance a farmer. He knows soil, weather, animals, stores, what a winter costs. Put him in a monastery and he should be reading the room like a man who has run a farmstead: how much food is here, who's been fed, is the roof sound, is that man's robe worth more than the plough. His trade is his eye.

### How to Characterize Without Explicit Internalization
1. Show the evidence used
2. Let the character act on the conclusion before it's warranted. Let the dialogue carry the accusation.
3. Let the scene contradict them. Either with definitive evidence during or after accusation
