He looked down to the tossed straw from the bed he had slept on. It was thrown about the floor. The bed itself has it's blankets half-removed. Diogo scowled, and then leaned over to start picking up his mess.

One by one, he would pick up large stalks and collect them into piles of handful, and with each handful he would place them back into the wooden frame. It took a few handfuls, but eventually the scowl left his face and was replaced by a song. 

A soft druidic hymn. The words danced off his lips smoother than any honeyed mead across the Steps, and flowed stronger than the rums of the southern coasts. 

ᚦᛖ ᛃᛟᚢᚾᚷ ᛋᛏᚨᚷ ᚷᚱᚨᛉᛖᛞ. The young stag grazed.
ᛏᚺᛖ ᚡᚨᛋᛏ ᚾᛖᚹ ᚷᚱᛟᚢᛖ. The vast new grove.
ᚠᚱᛟᛗ ᛏᚺᛖ ᛟᛚᛞ ᛗᛖᚨᛞᛟᚹ. From the old meadow.
ᛏᚺᛖ ᛗᛟᛏᚺᛖᚱ ᚲᚱᛁᛖᛞ. The mother cried.
ᛏᚺᛖ ᛋᛏᚨᚷ ᚷᚱᛖᚹ ᛟᛚᛞᛖᚱ. The stag grew older.
ᛏᚺᛖ ᚡᚨᛋᛏ ᚾᛖᚹ ᚷᚱᛟᚢᛖ. The vast new grove.
ᛏᚺᛖ ᚡᚨᛋᛏ ᚾᛖᚹ ᚷᚱᛟᚢᛖ, The vast new grove.

The words finished as Diogo picked up the last piece of straw and placed it into the mattress, covering it with the blanket. He brushed his hands off and stood over the mattress, thinking his work to be good enough. He turned around and began leaving the room, passing by the dozen other clean beds in this room. 

Diogo went towards the owner of the house's room and knocked on the door. No response. Diogo let out a small sigh of relief, and laid a few silver coins beneath the door. It was more than agreed upon, but likely not more than deserved. The boarding house was quite empty today and were getting emptier by the day in Amberwater, but people like her still needed jobs.

Going back to the room, Diogo grabbed his backpack and set it upon the newly cleaned bed. He began to undo the various complicated straps needed to open the main pouch of his back. On top, there was two large belts that attached to straps at the bottom of the bag. Underneath, there were four small strings that each were tied via surgeon's knots that attached to metal loops. At the top laid his messily strewn brewer's supplies that he quickly packed the night before. 

Diogo, quite flustered by his seeming lack of memory, scowled at the large stain of alcohol that soiled his newly purchased shirt. 

"Right before the big fuckin' day too," he groaned, stripping his current clothes off before nevertheless changing into the new outfit. It was nice and light, covering up his torso and going down to his bicep. He had not shown most of his arms this past decade, but he had not worked with the Lords Consortium either. 

It was about time. Diogo packed his bags and left the boarding house.

A chilled wind flowed into the boarding house through the horn window. It caressed around his arm, flowing up his sleeve into his clothes. The sleazy merchant he had met in town yesterday had said that this garment was perfect for the cold. When purchasing it, the layers seemed thick enough but seemingly, it does not account for the wind. 

Diogo embraced the cold as punishment.
