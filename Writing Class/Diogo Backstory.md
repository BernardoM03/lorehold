
## Virgil and the Birth of Diogo

Diogo was born in 1473 A.C in Virgil, a small farmstead on the fringes of Amberwater. Diogo was the first born child of Nicole and Ianson. They were honest workers, and through a combination of legacy, luck, and personality, they were well-respected names of the region. Because of this, Diogo was fortunate to grow up outside the commonality of poverty and be instilled with the virtues of honor, empathy, and community.

## The Death of Duke Titos, Edward the Exiled, and the War of Succession

The year was 1491 A.C. News was spreading throughout the Steps. “King Stavros is on his deathbed, and his successors are beginning to claim the throne for themselves.” Whispers of “The Game” spread even to the fields of Amberwater and the community of Virgil. Diogo’s best friend Leo was enamored by every rumor he heard, and would update Diogo with everything.

Diogo, on the other hand, could not bother himself with such things. He had just turned ten and eight. He was an adult now, his father’s condition was continuing to diminish, and his siblings were too young for the heavy fieldwork. For the next few months, Diogo and his mother found themselves buried with work. New king or not, the fields and animals weren’t going to stop needing work.

The harvest had not even started when the news of Duke Titos’ murder spread like a locust through Maerta. Even Diogo, known for his seriousness and responsibility, could not hold back his intrigue when Leo told him the news. The death of Duke Titos? He wasn’t exactly a prosperous or fair lord, and Stepan would definitely be the better successor, but such sudden death? That laid upon his mind like a hoe after a full day’s plowing. The air was sharper than usual, and the animals quieter. Diogo did not like how this “game” was heading.

“EDWARD THE EXILED HAS ARRIVED ON MAERTA” Leo screamed from across the fields. He ran towards Diogo as fast as he could, avoiding the harvest crops as he was reminded to do so more often than he’d liked to admit. “Diogo…” Leo muttered, catching his breath. “Edward the Exiled, has docked and set foot on Maerta. ON the seat of the late Duke. He means to rally his support HERE.” Only a week had passed since news of Duke Titos’ murder spread to Virgil. It was only a few more weeks before Diogo found himself standing before Edward himself.

It was a cold and cloudy day in Virgil. Most crops have been harvested, and Diogo was making sure the food stores and animals were ready for winter. It was quite the standard day for Diogo, with the exception that the banners of the crown could be seen crested over the rolling hills. Diogo and his mother made sure to keep the younger children in the main house. After that, they stood outside to receive who they correctly guessed this might be. Edward the Exiled.

“I am here to meet Ianson of Virgil,” the prince claimed. The air was silent and still. Diogo had never met a nobleman before. Sure, his father brought him to meetings with the lords of Amberwater, but a prince? Someone who could become King of the Steps? Diogo was beyond words. Diogo’s mother Nicole spoke. “My husband is sick, Your Grace. Too sick to speak. If there is something Your Grace would wish to converse concerning Virgil, my son and I would be honored to help.

“Y-yes. Of course Y-your Grace. I a-agree with my mother. If there’s anything Y-your Grace needs, we are here to serve”

“What a diligent lady and young lad,” said Edward. “Very well then. Please prepare a table, and then we may speak.” Diogo obeyed.

Their conversation went by quick enough, but each second felt like minutes for Diogo. Everything about the prince intrigued him.  His regal appearance and stature. The way he spoke with his accent and strange words. The conversation had only consisted of provisioning food and resources for the future months, but it felt like so much more to Diogo. He wouldn’t come here himself if it was just for that. That was when Diogo asked what could have been the most important question of his life. A Pandora’s Box to a world outside of Virgil. 

“May I ask an intruding question, Your Grace?” The prince nodded, looking curiously. “You must be here for a reason besides the resources. Otherwise you would have sent an envoy. Why are you here?” The moment the words left his mouth, his throat swelled up. Diogo you stupid cow, WHY did you ask that.

“You think this beneath me?” questioned the prince, a single eyebrow raising.

“No,” Diogo said quickly, swallowing. “I think it is too important for someone else.” Diogo held his breath in anticipation, hoping he did not anger the prince. All he heard was laughter. 

“You are a bright young lad. In a farmstead no less, but perhaps that's why you are so blunt.” He claimed, barely able to contain any more laughter. “I come here knowing that men will choose to follow and stand with me.” He took a long look into Diogo’ eyes. “Perhaps you will too someday.”

Diogo’ mother had been tense for the whole conversation, but the thought of that made her stand up. “My son has duties here, Your Grace.”

“Of course, and may he return to them stronger than before. If he chooses to…” he claimed, quickly peering to Diogo. He stood up and began to take his leave. “May the Steps know of Virgil as a hearth”. He looked to Diogo once more. “I know someone who might be interested in taking a squire if they are as interesting as you. I will have my clerics send a letter and relay it back here.” 

Diogo had not let go of his breath until the door was shut. He did not know what he felt in that moment for it was a rush he had never experienced before. The outstretched hand of the prince, the horizon of the sky beaming through the open door. For the first time in his life, Diogo had imagined how big the world could really be. The wonderful people the world could hold.

The following days had felt like the longest days of his life.  The first frost clung to the fields like a warning, and the boredom left behind by the harvest felt heavier than it ever had before. Diogo had almost convinced himself it had meant nothing. 

Until the rider came. 

He wore dark leathers that seemed as if they were unwashed for weeks. He introduced himself as Ser Alric Ven of Tomton. A hardy sailor of a man, and swore like one too. Diogo found him repulsive.
## The War of Succession and Squireship

In the months of squireship, Diogo had grown accustomed to Ser Alric as they travelled the island of Maerta under the banner of Prince Edward. He was foul-mouthed and graceless. He drilled Diogo until his arms and feet stopped working, and then drilled him further. He called him "a bright little fucker" like a more polite man might say "son," which Diogo suspected was as close as Ser Alric could get to the word. Diogo grew to love that part of his master. He also grew to love the art of swordfighting, which came quite easily for him, or rather it occupied more of his mind than most.

Then the call for battle came. It had been months since King Stavros died and the battle between Denas and Edward, and the horns of war once again rang throughout Maerta. "Seems like the roach Denas didn't learn his lesson the first time," was how Ser Alric received the letter. He handed it to Diogo without reading the second page. Diogo held onto the letter for much longer.

Diogo could only think of Edward. He had never stopped thinking about him. It was a shameful thing, he knew, but the memory of that afternoon in Virgil had not dimmed. He had constructed, over these months of marching and drilling and sleeping in cold camps, a very particular version of the Prince that he carried with him like a second sword.  

The battle was nothing like he had imagined.

He had imagined it like the ways that Ser Alric had described his previous battles; Significant. That there would be a structure to it. A moment where you could look around and understand what was happening and why. Instead there was noise and blood and men and the sea. Soldiers he did not recognize were dying in ways he could never imagine, and without time to process or understand. He stayed near Alric, and relied on his training. That was all he knew how to do. 

Out of the corner of his eye, a crossbow bolt hit Alric. The bolt had come from a pirate with copper skin, a scar across his neck, and a shark-tooth necklace, who seemed to be shooting soldiers indiscriminately. For all the months Alric had boasted about how grand his death would be, he went down without ceremony. There was no moment. No last stand, no exchange of blows. Because of that, Diogo didn’t believe that he had really died. 

Diogo ran to his master. He did not know what he intended to do when he got there, but he felt he had to be there. Alric's eyes were open but life had left them. Diogo laid upon the deck and screamed his name trying to wake him up until something struck him from behind, sending him flying.

He crashed into the cold and briny depths of the Narrow Straits. It was so dark that he did not know which direction was up. He felt like he was crying but the water of the sea hid that from him too. He did not struggle. He was too tired and too empty to struggle.

He woke up on the shore with the taste of salt, blood, and sand. Petrels on the rocks nearby seemed to land upon the shipwrecks beside him, watching him intently. The sounds of the battle had moved somewhere else, or perhaps ended. He could not tell. He did not care. Diogo gripped the sand in his fist and curled up into a ball.

Ser Alric was dead. He knew that. He just needed a moment. Perhaps the prince would forgive him for taking just this moment.
## The Aftermath

Edward had won the battle. The pretenders’ fleet had broken in the Narrow Straits, with both Denas and Steppan dead or missing. The Steps were Edward's. He was to be King. At least that was what everyone said, in the camps.

Diogo sat with the other injured men at the camps, waiting for medicine to arrive and dispatches to be sent. The petrels had also been there since the shore. They were there when he closed his eyes, when he opened them. No one else seemed to notice them. Only when the nurse would do their rounds, offer alcohol to those with too much pain, would the petrels disappear. Diogo hated alcohol. The stink on Alric’s breath each day had disgusted him for months. Now, it was the only way he could be alone enough to mourn him.

Finally, the day came. A messenger passed by the tent for a dispatch in honor of Ser Alric’s fight to the end. This would be recorded in the history books. Ser Alric Ven of Tomton received two lines in a dispatch.  Ser Arlic Ven of Tomton, killed in an engagement at the Narrow Straits. Loyal in service under His Grace Edward’s banner. Diogo read the line three times, making sure it wasn’t his eyes playing tricks on him. They had spelled Alric incorrectly. 

The lord Houran of Eastwatch,  was responsible for the betrayal of Stepan from inside. He was received in Edward's hall within the fortnight and left with his lands intact. Wex Pyke, the despicable pirate captain whose own men were the scum that killed Ser Alric, was also received and left with a full pardon. Ser Alric had fought on the vanguard of the battle, and left dead with his name to be misremembered in the history books. 

It was a sick joke.

He had spent months carrying this perfect version of Edward he had built in Virgil. He had carried it through leaving home and the drilling and the cold and the battle and the death of Ser Alric and his weeks of recovery and waiting because it contained a memory of a good man who could show Diogo the greatness of the world and that he could be a part of it and do good things. He had told himself that when this war was over, when Edward had won, there would be…

Diogo could not finish the thought. Edward had won. Edward had won and rewarded a traitor and a monster, and ignored Diogo and Ser Alric. The petrels swarmed around him more.

Diogo became aware that he was sitting on the ground. He did not remember sitting down. He could only remember Ser Alric saying bright little fucker in that particular tone that meant he was satisfied with the day's work. He could remember his mother standing up from the table when Edward suggested he might leave. He could remember his father's condition, which had been diminishing when he left and had presumably continued to diminish without his help. He could remember the copper-skinned man with the scar and shark-toothed necklace. He could remember the water, and the dark, and something that had looked back at him from a place that did not have a direction. The petrels swarmed closer.

He pressed the heels of his hands into his eyes until the world went to white. When he took them away the birds were still there and he was crying 

He sat in the dirt of the victorious camp and he wept for Ser Alric Ven of Tomton, who would be remembered for two lines and a misspelled name, and for the afternoon in Virgil, which had been real and had also meant nothing, and for actually believing, only if just for a few months, that the world had cracked open just for him.
