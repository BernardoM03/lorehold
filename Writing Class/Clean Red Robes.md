"You've got to be fucking kidding me."

The Elder Monk's eyes were looking down upon Diogo. He was as unflinching as the limestone statue behind him. "You are to be gone before night's fall." 

"Elder, please..." Diogo pleaded through his clenched teeth. "I've only a few days before I'm done with the library texts. After that, I'll have the notes I need and you'll never see me again."

Unflinching. "I am afraid you don't understand. The light of knowledge is not something that can be shown to sinners like you. There is no exchange. You had a privilege, and have now lost it." 

The Elder Monk's voice was soft and firm, which reminded Diogo of... His eyes shifted away from the Elder Monk. He saw the novice monk standing* behind him like boy behind his mother's skirt. Diogo clenched his fist until his chest tightened and the ringing got louder. 

The Elder Monk noticed this. "Do not blame Brother Franco for your vices, Brother Diogo. He confessed his sin and was cleansed by the flame." He leaned closer to him, placing his hand upon his cheek like fathers do in children's stories. They were worker's hands sticking through clean sleeves. "You have refused, and thus blaspheme the Morninglord himself." His breath faintly smelled of fish.

The Elder's Monk's woolen red robes blurred into his face as Diogo held back a wretch. He stepped away from the Elder Monk's embrace to give himself space and started to take a deep breath. As his eyes were closed, he could hear Franco whispering to the Elder Monk, like how his younger siblings used to. The ringing in his ears and tightness in his chest grew.

Diogo struggled a laugh under his smile. "Y'know what Elder?"

The Elder Monk leaned forward, flicking his eyebrow up in response.

Diogo cleared his throat. "Inside you, I see, peasant who only accepted this light of the Morninglord so that he could free himself from the rough worker's life." 

The Elder Monk leaned as far back as he just leaned forward, eyes agape. Diogo took that opportunity to step forward closer.

"I see a sorry old man who wishes to save face after a dumb rich merchant's boy, whose parents couldn't even bother to take care of him, admitted only now, after needing to pay up 15 gold pieces. Not two weeks ago when I paid him."

Franco shrunk even further behind the Elder Monk, his mouth trembling open and eyes watering with tears at the battled hardened veteran approaching him. Diogo put one hand on each of their faces. "I see a coward who could only think of his principles and self. I'll remind you Elder. You deny me the ability to study history that could save thousands of lives here in Maerta."

The Elder Monk was still, staring into Diogo's eyes for nearly a half minute. Finally, he spoke. 

"I believe you to be a smart man, Brother Diogo, but you have a vivid imagination." His tone was no longer soft. He grabbed Diogo's hand and carefully brushed it aside. "My mind was made up the moment I approached you. You are excused." His eyes lingered in contact with Diogo's for a few seconds more, before sharply strutting passed. 

Diogo's shoulder's dropped down and his arms hung. The ringing grew. Quickly, he turned around to state his next argument to the Elder Monk, but the mud on the soles of his boots stopped Diogo.
